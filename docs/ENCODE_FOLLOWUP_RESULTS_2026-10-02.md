# ENCODE attribution: follow-up audit (2 October 2026)

This document records the results produced after the [completed focused campaign](ENCODE_RESULTS_2026-09-29.md). It separates finished analyses from partially analysed or unfinished runs. Exact per-seed, per-mask and per-set values remain in `outputs/encode_atlas_v1/followup/`; the CSV files, rather than rounded values here, are the source for figures. These follow-ups were selected after inspection of the focused results and should be labelled exploratory or robustness analyses in the paper.

The other four mask fractions also favor functional 256. A seed-42 run summary reports MSE **0.017534**, but has no paired analysis yet. | The analysis CSV contains only seed 17. | 2. | 3. Assay specificity and proxy null | In a chromosome-blocked ridge proxy, 100 repeated prevalence-bin-matched histone/non-histone draws favor histones for mean-beta MSE by **8.85%** on average (2.5–97.5% range of draw effects: 6.13–12.07% reduction), whereas label-blind draws average **+0.18%**. All 100 real draws favor histones. A separate single-seed post-hoc decoder gives MSE **0.017643** for context+TF and **0.017740** for context+DNase, each better than context alone but worse than the confirmed context+histone range **0.015951–0.015986**. Single-seed full-minus-TF and full-minus-DNase runs have MSE **0.015646** and **0.015807**, respectively. | **Proxy analysis complete; decoder confirmation incomplete.** The proxy's permutation comparison is descriptive for track-subset draws, not a causal assay-identity test. TF/DNase decoder arms are post-hoc, seed 17 only; their differences from histones have no three-seed confirmation. See `followup/exp3/tables/proxy_null_summary.csv`, `proxy_ridge_null.csv`, `posthoc_add_test.csv`, and `posthoc_eval/*/summary.json`. |
| 4. Excluded-chromosome recomputation | A fresh calculation from saved predictions reproduces the original three-seed 50% masking ordering: functional **0.123681–0.124140**, compacted CpGPT-large **0.129945–0.130178**, context+histones **0.124645–0.125334**, context **0.133629–0.134066**. | **Recomputation complete** for existing arms; the configured native-512 CpGPT and native-128 DeepCpG chromosome-transfer runs have no output here. | 5. Repeated matched EWAS and clock probes | With 20 matched-negative draws, functional 256 beats native CpGPT-large on **7/13** evaluable sets under base matching, and **8/13** when matching also considers array probe type. It beats native DeepCpG on **11/13** in either variant. CpGPT-large is better on age, Horvath clock and PhenoAge clock; functional 256 is better on colorectal and prostate cancer sets and smoking. For base matching, colorectal ΔAUC (functional minus native CpGPT) is **+0.141**; Horvath is **−0.161**. | **Complete robustness analysis.** It confirms that external set membership is mixed, even with native FM dimensions and repeated matching. Three candidate sets lack matched support. These AUCs measure recovery of listed CpGs versus matched unlisted CpGs, not clinical prediction; the same TCGA CpG universe underlies all probes. See `followup/exp5/protocol.json`, `summary_auc_delta.csv`, `evaluable_sets.csv`, `matching_summary_per_seed.csv`, `probe_type_balance.csv`, and `oof_predictions.parquet`. |

All paths in the table are relative to `outputs/encode_atlas_v1/`. The repeated matched EWAS analysis uses fold-wise standardization and logistic regression with chromosome GroupKFold; its uncertainty combines matching draws and within-chromosome pair resampling. Matching draws are not independent biological replicates. The proxy null's 2.5–97.5% range describes variation across sampled track subsets, not a confidence interval for a population effect.

The principal paper conclusion remains the focused campaign's held-out-patient result: functional reference annotations beat the tested frozen locus embeddings, and histone tracks explain much of the measured gain beyond the context block. The follow-ups strengthen the dimensionality, decodability and matching checks, but also make the scope clearer: CpGPT retains advantages for specific external clock/age sets, and genomic transfer has high absolute error. The unfinished native-dimension decoder and chromosome-transfer runs should not be described as completed evidence.
